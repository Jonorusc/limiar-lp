Why it exists

“Will charge to 80%.”
Charges to 100%.

On macOS 27 the built-in charge limit often doesn't hold, and the apps that used to fix it — AlDente, batt, Battery Toolkit — stopped working when Apple removed the hardware keys they relied on. Limiar uses the one that's left.

Features

Everything a charge limit should be. Nothing else.

Your range

Stop at 80%, resume at 75% — or any levels you like, set with one slider.

MagSafe tells you

Green while Limiar holds the charge. Orange while it charges. No need to look at the screen.

Lives in the menu bar

A battery icon with the percentage inside, in the same colour as the MagSafe light.

100% when you need it

Heading out? Charge to full once. The limit comes back when you unplug.

Awake while you sleep

Lid closed overnight? Limiar plans ahead so it never overshoots while your Mac sleeps.

Fails safe

If anything stops Limiar, normal charging comes straight back. Open source, MIT licensed.

How it works

A threshold, not a timer.

  1. Charges to your limit

    Plugged in below the limit, your Mac charges normally.

  2. Holds the line

    At the limit, Limiar disconnects wall power through the SMC. Your Mac runs on battery, still plugged in.

  3. Resumes on cue

    When the battery drifts down to your resume level, power comes back and the cycle repeats.

Under the hood: macOS 27 removed CHTE, CH0B and CH0C and locked the firmware charge band. Limiar uses CHIE, which cuts adapter input, from a small root service registered with SMAppService.

Install

Up and running in a minute.

  1. 1Download the .dmg and drag Limiar to Applications.
  2. 2First launch: right-click › Open. Limiar is free and not notarized by Apple.
  3. 3Click Turn On Charge Limit and allow Limiar in System Settings.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Is it safe for my Mac?

Limiar only switches wall power on and off — the same thing that happens when you unplug the cable. If the service stops for any reason, it restores normal charging first.

Why does it need permission to run in the background?

Talking to the battery controller requires administrator privileges. Limiar keeps that part in a tiny service you approve once in System Settings; the app itself runs as you.

Which Macs are supported?

MacBooks with Apple Silicon on macOS 26 or later. It's built for macOS 27 and tested on a MacBook Air M4. If it doesn't work on yours, please open an issue.

Why isn't it on the App Store?

The App Store doesn't allow apps that control hardware like this. Limiar is distributed on GitHub, free, with all the source code.

How do I uninstall it?

Open Settings… › Background service › Remove…, then move Limiar to the Trash. Charging goes back to normal immediately.
